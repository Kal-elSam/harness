# Cost-diversified team
Objective: team formation assigns roles by each model's quota cost, not by model name, so expensive models only take roles that need them. Branch: `delivery/kairo-runtime-clean`. Runner: `node --test`.

## Specs
- S1: "no te bases en nombres de modelos, basate en cuato cobra cada modelo, claramente modelso de ultima genracion como astra cobra mas caro que un haiku, entonces la medicioon no es nombre es cuaota como tal". Each candidate model carries a quota-cost weight derived from provider data (price / quota consumption), never from name matching.
- S2: "para que pondremos a astra a hacer test? [...] esa misma tarea podrian hacerlo los modelos mas baratos de claude o codex". Low-demand roles (e.g. tester, explorer) go to the cheapest model that meets the role's aptitude floor; high-cost models are reserved for roles whose floor only they meet (diversification).
- S3: "tambien considera cuota, esfuerzo, porcentaje de la cuota disponible". Available quota % per provider weighs the choice.
- S4 (assumption): the analyst model stays chosen manually by the user ("la eleccion de modelo para el analisis del proyecto lo hace el usuario manualmente").

- S5: Delegation re-routes on quota (Sam chose A): when a task is delegated and the role's assigned provider is exhausted or under pressure, route it to the next-cheapest model that meets that role's aptitude floor, and tell the user about the change.
- S6: Pace to the reset: "la idea es que podamos identificar cuanto nos queda de cuota, contexto y saber a que hrs es el receteo de cada proveedor y con eso en mente diversificar y administrar bien para que nunca dejemos de trabajar; la idea de que lleguemos a 0% antes del receteo no es buena porque tendriamos que esperar a que se recee". Example values: "Context 73% left · 5h 95% left · weekly 41% left · Codex 5h 38% · wk 90% left". Every window (5h, weekly) is judged by remaining % against time left until its reset (burn pace), not raw % alone; a provider burning faster than its reset allows becomes more expensive, one with surplus before reset becomes cheaper.
- S7 (assumption): "Context % left" is the orchestrator session's context, not provider quota; it is surfaced alongside quota in the routing explanation but does not change provider cost weights in this unit.

- S8: Per-role demand tiers. Demanding roles (Architect, Debugger, Reviewer) require a strong model; light roles (Explorer, Tester) accept a cheap one; Builder is standard. Each tier has its own absolute quality/aptitude floor (not a relative "~93% of QUALITY" retention), and within a tier the cheapest qualified model wins (S1-S3 unchanged). Live evidence 2026-10-15: the relative floor let glm-5.3-flash win Explorer/Architect/Builder/Debugger/Tester.
- S9: Unknown quota is a last resort: a provider whose quota cannot be read (e.g. OpenCode Go usage HTTP 403) never beats a qualified candidate from a provider with known, healthy quota; it is used only when no known-quota candidate qualifies.
- S10: "lo limitamos por ahora a claude y codex": team formation candidates are limited to the claude and codex providers through one configurable allowlist (default ["claude","codex"]), not by deleting other providers' code.

- S11: "debemos sacar provecho a todos los proveedores y modelos disponibles, por que de que me serviria entonces usar 4 de codex y uno de claude, estoy recargando el trabajo solo a un lado". Team formation balances the workload across allowlisted providers in proportion to each provider's usable quota (remaining share and reset pace, S6), so no provider carries most roles while another has headroom; cost (S1-S3) and role demand floors (S8) still apply within that balance. Live evidence 2026-10-15: 4 codex roles + 2 claude Haiku roles.

- S12: Reviewer independence stays a hard gate (pre-existing policy "S5 R1", tests in test/model-intelligence.test.js "Reviewer blocks visibly when no independent provider is eligible" and "buildAiTeam never forces Reviewer onto a decisively worse independent alternative"): Reviewer must come from a provider different from Builder's; with no eligible independent candidate, or only a decisively worse one, Reviewer is blocked visibly, never assigned to the Builder's provider.
- S13 (assumption, parent calibration from live scores): demanding floor 0.60 instead of 0.65. Live Architect/Reviewer scores: Claude Opus 5.5 .6415, Codex gpt-6-astra .6595, gpt-5.6-terra .6135, Sonnet 5.5 .5800. A .65 floor excluded Opus 5.5 by .009 while admitting Astra by .010, a margin inside evidence noise, which defeats S11.

- S14: Project profile drives role demand: technologies, architecture, complexity and risks from the project analysis (project-analysis.js / computeProjectProfile) change each role's required capabilities and demand floor for that project, instead of stopping at the picker's context string.
- S15: Official vendor benchmark snapshots (official-benchmark-snapshots.js: gpqa-diamond, terminal-bench) count as scored evidence when AA lacks that metric for a model, with their lower trust (verified:false) reflected in weight, not ignored.
- S16: Kairo run telemetry (cost, duration, success) is attributed per role and per model and feeds role scoring and efficiency, not only a per-model tiebreak.
- S17: Owned role-aptitude evidence gets a production producer (role-aptitude runner on a real path). It spends quota, so it only runs when Sam explicitly authorizes a run; never automatically.
- S18: Effort-benchmark outcomes (scripts/effort-eval/outcome-tasks/results.jsonl) feed a low-weight per-model prior; small sample, never decisive.

- S19: "el mas barato gana? se supone que hay roles importantes como orquestador y arquitecto o reviews por ejemplo, obvio lo barato es importante pero no toma el mayor peso, asi que si debe ser mas fuerte acorde al rol". Within a tier the pick is a role-weighted trade-off between capability and quota-adjusted cost, not cheapest-qualified: demanding roles (Architect, Reviewer, Debugger, and the orchestrator if it is a team role) weight capability far above cost; light roles (Explorer, Tester) weight cost highest; Builder in between. Provider balance (S11), floors (S8/S13/S14), unknown-quota last resort (S9) and Reviewer independence (S12) still apply. Supersedes "cheapest qualified wins" in S2/S8.

- S20: Incomplete evidence competes with a penalty, never excludes: when a model lacks a benchmark that a role requires (e.g. claude-opus-5-5 has no AA codingIndex), it competes using the required signals it does have, with an explicit incomplete-evidence penalty and a reason naming the missing signal, instead of being dropped from the role pool. A model with none of a role's required signals stays excluded.

- S21: "ves toda la inforamcion que tenmos disponible dentro de AA?? creo que no estamos uasndolo adecuadamente todo". Cost per task replaces price per token: read AA's documented endpoint /api/v2/language/models/free (paginated; read every page, 4 pages / ~800 models on 2026-10-16) and use artificial_analysis_intelligence_index_cost.cost_per_task.total_cost as the model cost signal when present; price per MTok stays the fallback. Effort variants (slug suffixes -low/-medium/-high/-xhigh) map to the same Kairo model when Kairo cannot pick effort. Live evidence: Opus 5 xhigh $4.88/task vs Sonnet 5.5 medium $0.48/task, while output price is only 2.5x apart. Keep the legacy endpoint for per-benchmark scores.
- S22: Role work volume weighs cost: "alargar la vida util de nuestras suscripciones? tanto apr also que tienen solo una como para los que tienen multiples". A role's cost weight scales with how much work it does, so high-volume roles (Builder) prefer efficient models and expensive models go to low-volume deciding roles (Architect, Reviewer, Debugger). Assumption until S16 telemetry exists: relative volume Builder 1.0, Tester 0.6, Explorer 0.5, Debugger 0.3, Architect 0.2, Reviewer 0.2; replace with measured per-role tokens when available. Live 2026-10-16: Claude 80% / Codex 10% scenario makes Opus 5.5 Builder.
- S23: "podemos lelgar al objetivo de nunca llegar a 0% de uso de cuota?" Quota pressure also moves demanding roles: as a provider's paced pressure (S6) rises, every role's weight shifts toward cost, and a provider projected to hit 0% before its reset keeps a reserve (assumption: 10% of the tightest window) by downshifting its roles to cheaper qualified models before exhausting it. Applies to single-provider users (downshift within the provider) and multi-provider users (shift to the provider with headroom). Live 2026-10-16: Codex at 10% still keeps Astra in Architect/Debugger/Reviewer; Claude at 10% gets Opus 5.5 as Reviewer.
- S24: "esto lo vamos a dejar publico para el que quiera usarlo, por que lo que la seguridad es muy importante sobrne mis prpopios datos y accesos". Each user brings their own AA key (env only), the key never appears in logs, errors, cache or selection evidence (tested), no AA data snapshot is committed, and surfaces showing AA numbers credit Artificial Analysis.

- S25: Single-provider Claude users get strong demanding roles: in a Claude-only pool, Architect and Debugger must not go to Claude Haiku 4.5 when Claude Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 clear the demanding floor; thin-but-real evidence (provisional, S20) must not lose to a broader but weaker model on breadth alone. Live 2026-10-16: Claude-only scenario puts Haiku 4.5 in Architect/Debugger.
- S26: The high-volume Builder never lands on the most expensive qualified model of a provider when a cheaper qualified one exists (S21/S22). Live 2026-10-16: Codex-only and Claude 10% scenarios make gpt-6-astra Builder; find and fix the cause.
- S27: Architect and Explorer always exist: "si, avanza con esos 4" (approving: Architect and Explorer always present). The analyst can add capabilities to them but never removes them. Live 2026-10-16: Kairo trial lost Architect and Explorer.
- S28: One model is one candidate: dated aliases of the same model (claude-haiku-4-5 and claude-haiku-4-5-20251001) collapse into a single candidate. Live 2026-10-16: Haiku 4.5 listed twice.

- S29: AA agentic index (already fetched in C10, artificial_analysis_agentic_index, free tier) becomes a scored signal for multi-step tool-using roles: "Conectar Agentic Index para Planner, Orchestrator, Builder y DevOps". In Kairo roles: Architect (orchestrator), Builder, Debugger; Explorer low weight. Missing agentic index follows S20 (penalty, not exclusion).
- S30: AA latency is a scored signal only for short interactive roles: "Conectar tiempo de respuesta solo para Tester, Verifier y Documenter" (Kairo: Tester). Use median_end_to_end_response_time_seconds or output speed from the documented endpoint; Architect and Reviewer ignore time.
- S31: The cheapest capable model must not lose on missing evidence alone: gpt-6-luna (price 0.1/0.5, sciCode .546 vs Terra .55) never wins Explorer because it lacks reasoning and instruction-following benchmarks. Reproduce the cause and make thin-but-real evidence compete under S20 for light roles; Explorer must not reward benchmark breadth over role fit and cost.

- S33: Per-role metric weights replace equal averaging of a role's required capabilities: "Ponderar por rol, no promediar igual". Starting weights over signals Kairo has (coding, agentic, terminal, time, cost), renormalized when a signal is missing; a future own-reliability signal (S16/S17) gets weight only when measured data exists. Cost stays governed by S19/S22/S23 (volume and quota pressure), not a fixed share.

- S34: Builder (standard tier) floor drops from .60 to .55 for every capability mix, provisional until own runs (C8): "Baja a 0.55 ahora" (Sam's pasted guidance: "0.55 habilita a Terra/Sol como Builder, pero no elimina a Astra"). Project risk raises still lift it; demanding (.60) and light (.35) floors unchanged; the S26 coding-only discount is superseded. Escalation to a stronger Builder after failures or for high-risk diffs is runtime delegation (S5) and is out of scope here.

- S35: "como es posuiboloe que en poryectos mas demandante en genral usemos terra y en el menos demandante astra? [...] emma, es un proeycto de asistente personal imposadlo por ia, eso deberia de entenderlo elk analista qdel poreycto apra determinar al equipo, a difernecia de cva-page que es una paginam simple de lading page". The analyst returns a structured demand assessment: product purpose (from README/docs) and risk categories, each rated 1-5 with cited evidence: executes actions or touches security/permissions, money, personal data, real-time or concurrency, AI/LLM integration, domain complexity. That assessment, not the presence of tooling (lint, tests, layered-architecture docs), drives role floors and required capabilities. - S38: Detect Swift projects: Package.swift implies build (swift build) and test (swift test) commands and the Swift stack.

- S39: Critical project demand never sets an unreachable floor: demanding floors are capped at .65, and at critical demand the demanding roles (Architect, Debugger, Reviewer) take the strongest available qualified model with capability-dominant weights (cost weight near zero, quota reserve S23 still applies). Sam approved: "si". Live 2026-10-16: the .70 critical floor was met by no model, so Emma's Reviewer fell back to gpt-5.6-terra.
- S40: "necesitamos roles y mas roles, eso hace la diferencia" (Sam approved the catalog with "si"). Roles activate from the project analysis instead of all being always on:
  - Explorer, Architect: always (S27). Architect stays the orchestrator; Planner never replaces it.
  - Frontend Builder + Backend Builder: when the project has a UI and an API/server; they replace the single Builder (standard tier, Builder volume split between them).
  - Planner: domainComplexity >= 3 (standard, low volume); breaks the Architect plan into tasks.
  - Security Reviewer: actionsAndSecurity, money or personalData >= 4 (demanding, low volume); provider independence from the Builder(s) like Reviewer (S12).
  - DevOps: CI, Docker or deploy configuration detected (standard, terminal-weighted, low volume).
  - Verifier: a test command exists (light, high volume); only runs tests and lint and reports evidence.
  - Refactorer: domainComplexity >= 4 (standard, medium volume).
  - Documenter: a docs folder exists or the project is a published library (light, low volume).
  - Tester, Debugger, Reviewer: unchanged rules, including gap roles (S36).
  Each new role has a role profile (objective, allowed actions from the closed vocabulary, escalation signals), capabilities, metric weights (S33), tier floor, volume (S22) and near-equivalence band; the analyst vocabulary includes every catalog role; new roles count toward the provider cap and are editable in the team edit catalog.
- S41: One role registry is the single source of truth for every per-role attribute (capabilities, metric weights, tier, volume, band, profile, activation rule); KNOWN_ROLES, TECHNICAL_ROLES, tier lists, ROLE_WORK_VOLUME, ROLE_NEAR_EQUIVALENCE_BAND, ROLE_METRIC_WEIGHTS and the analyst vocabulary derive from it. Delivered first with no behavior change.

- S42: Demand calibration (Sam: "si aplica la calibracion"): high demand requires two or more verified categories at level 4 or more, or a verified mean of at least 3.5; a single category at 4 is not enough. Critical requires a verified category at 5 and a verified mean of at least 3.5. Run-to-run stability: when a new assessment differs from the stored assessment for the same project by at most one level per category, the stored levels are kept; larger differences replace them and the change is recorded. Live 2026-10-16: high triggered in 3 of 4 projects from actionsAndSecurity 4 alone, and cva-page flipped low to high between two runs (3 vs 4).
